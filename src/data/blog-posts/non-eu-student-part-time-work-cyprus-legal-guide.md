Finding part-time employment is essential for many international students in Cyprus to manage rent, living expenses, and grocery budgets. However, employment for third-country (Non-EU) students is strictly regulated by the Ministry of Labour and Social Insurance and the Civil Registry and Migration Department (CRMD). 

Working outside the permitted sectors or exceeding legal hour limits can lead to severe fines for employers and deportation or visa cancellation for students. 

In this comprehensive 2026 guide, we break down the **legal 20-hour work limit**, **actual minimum hourly wages**, **permitted job sectors**, and the **mandatory contract stamping process**.

## 1. Cyprus Student Minimum Wage & Hourly Pay Rates

Cyprus enforces a National Minimum Wage framework (€979 gross upon hire, rising to €1,088 gross after 6 months of continuous full-time service). However, because students work part-time on an hourly basis, compensation is calculated hourly.

* **At €6.00/hour:** 80 hours × €6.00 = **€480.00 gross / month**
* **At €7.50/hour:** 80 hours × €7.50 = **€600.00 gross / month**
* **At €8.50/hour (Delivery/Tips included):** 80 hours × €8.50 = **€680.00 gross / month**

> 💡 *Note on Deductions:* Registered legal employment deducts **8.3% for Social Insurance** and **2.65% for the General Healthcare System (GeSY)** directly from monthly wages. Student income falls far below the annual €19,500 personal income tax threshold, so no income tax is withheld.

## 3. Mandatory 4-Step Contract Stamping Process

You cannot start working immediately upon finding an employer. You must legalize your employment with the **District Labour Office** (Eparchiako Grafeio Ergasias) before logging your first shift:

1. **Obtain Standard Student Contract:** The employer provides the official 3-copy standard contract form issued by the Department of Labour.
2. **Attach Required Documents:**
   * Valid Passport and Pink Slip (or ARC / White Slip receipt).
   * University Timetable / Registration confirmation showing class hours (to prove working hours do not clash with lectures).
   * Employer's active Social Insurance registration number.
3. **Submit to District Labour Office:** Both employer and student details are verified. The labour officer stamps and approves the agreement.
4. **Register for Social Insurance (TIC/SIS):** Ensure your employer registers your social security contributions to receive free healthcare access under GeSY.

## 4. Delivery Courier Work (Wolt & Foody)

Food delivery remains the most popular flexible earning avenue for international students across Limassol, Nicosia, Larnaca, and Paphos:

* **Fleet Agency System:** Most international riders operate through third-party fleet partner agencies rather than direct contracts.
* **Agency Deductions:** Fleet agencies typically deduct an **18% to 20% management commission** alongside social insurance contributions.
* **Fuel & Scooter Expenses:** Fuel and scooter rentals are typically self-funded by the rider. Factoring in petrol (€1.40–€1.55/liter) and vehicle maintenance is essential for tracking actual net profit.
