Health insurance and medical care in Cyprus can feel overwhelming for newly arriving international students. While every non-EU student is legally required to hold private medical insurance for [Civil Registry and Migration Department (CRMD) Pink Slip residency](/blog/cyprus-student-visa-requirements-2026-guide/), few realize that standard student policies only cover specific emergencies and in-hospital admissions—not everyday clinic visits.

Moreover, many students are unaware of **GeSY (ΓεΣΥ - General Healthcare System)**: the state-run universal healthcare system that grants access to heavily subsidized or free doctor visits, specialist check-ups, and €1 prescription medications once you begin [legal part-time employment](/blog/non-eu-student-part-time-work-cyprus-legal-guide/).

This operational manual explains how to navigate your mandatory student policy, avoid unexpected medical bills, submit successful insurance claims, and register for GeSY as an international student.

## 1. Private Student Insurance vs. GeSY: The Core Differences

Understanding what your policy covers protects you from surprise medical debt.

| Healthcare Dimension | Private Student Insurance (Immigration) | GeSY Universal System (Working Students) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Eligibility** | Mandatory for all non-EU students | Non-EU students with active, declared employment |
| **Average Cost** | €120 – €180 per year (Upfront premium) | 2.65% gross payroll deduction (Social Insurance) |
| **GP / Routine Visits**| Generally **Excluded** or capped at €20/visit | **Free** visits with your registered Personal Doctor (GP) |
| **Prescription Medicine**| Pay out-of-pocket (Limited reimbursement) | **€1.00** flat co-payment per prescribed medication |
| **Specialist Referrals** | Requires pre-authorization letter | **€6.00** flat fee with GP referral |
| **Emergency Inpatient** | Covered up to policy limit (e.g., €8,500+) | Comprehensive emergency & ICU coverage |
| **Primary Use Case** | Visa issuance, residency renewal | Real daily healthcare, diagnostics, recurring care |

## 2. What Your Mandatory Immigration Insurance Actually Covers

Most colleges and universities arrange private policies through insurers like *Universal Life*, *Hydra*, *Minerva*, or *Altius Insurance*. By law, these immigration-standard student plans include:

* **Inpatient Hospitalization:** Coverage between €8,500 and €13,000 annually for emergency hospital stays, surgeries, and intensive care treatments.
* **Outpatient Emergency Care:** Limited emergency treatments (typically capped at €600 to €1,000 per policy year) following unexpected accidents.
* **Repatriation of Remains:** Up to €3,500 – €5,000 for transport expenses in critical circumstances.

### What is Strictly EXCLUDED?
1. Pre-existing medical conditions diagnosed prior to arrival.
2. Routine dental care (cleanings, fillings, wisdom teeth extraction) unless caused by accidental facial trauma.
3. Routine eye examinations, contact lenses, and corrective glasses.
4. STD tests, routine general check-ups, and non-emergency dermatology appointments.

## 3. How to Submit a Private Insurance Claim (Step-by-Step)

If you fall sick and require treatment at a private clinic or hospital, follow this precise procedure to get reimbursed:

### Step 1: Collect Itemized Invoices
Never leave the clinic with only a credit card slip. You must obtain:
* An official **Medical Report / Diagnosis Sheet** signed and stamped by the attending physician.
* An **Itemized Tax Invoice (Fiscal Receipt)** clearly indicating every medical service, injection, or test performed.

### Step 2: Download Your Insurer’s Claim Form
Visit your insurance provider’s official portal or request the claim document from your university's International Student Office. Fill out sections detailing your policy number, passport details, and incident date.

### Step 3: Physician Signature
Take the claim form back to the doctor or hospital administrative desk to get their stamp and signature confirming treatment.

### Step 4: Submission & Payout
Submit the original physical receipts and claim dossier to your university liaison or upload digital copies via your insurer’s web portal. Standard reimbursements are processed via direct IBAN wire transfer within **14 to 30 business days**. Ensure your [local bank account details](/blog/how-to-open-bank-account-cyprus-student-immigration-guide/) are up to date.

## 4. Are Non-EU Students Eligible for GeSY?

**Yes—provided you work legally in Cyprus.**

Under official Republic of Cyprus healthcare regulations, non-EU third-country nationals qualify as registered GeSY beneficiaries once they hold:
1. A valid student residence permit (Pink Slip) or temporary ARC receipt slip.
2. An active Social Insurance Number (TIC / Social Insurance registration).
3. Evidence of mandatory 2.65% health system contributions deducted from their legitimate part-time salary.

### How to Register on the GeSY Beneficiaries Portal:
1. Navigate to the official portal at `gesy.org.cy`.
2. Create an account utilizing your Alien Registration Certificate (ARC) number and passport details.
3. Search the registry for an English-speaking General Practitioner located in your district (e.g., Nicosia, Limassol, Larnaca, Paphos).
4. Confirm your selection. Your chosen GP will verify your registration request online within 48 hours.

## 5. What to Do in a Medical Emergency (ER Protocol)

If you face a sudden severe medical emergency, do not wait for insurance claims approval:

* **Cyprus Emergency Hotline:** Dial **112** (Universal European Emergency Number for Police, Ambulance, and Fire Service—toll-free).
* **General Public Hospital Emergency Rooms (TAEP):** Every major city features a government general hospital with a 24/7 emergency unit (Nicosia General, Limassol General, Larnaca General, and Paphos General).
* **Emergency Fee:** Under standard non-beneficiary regulations, an emergency room triage entry fee of **€10** applies. Present your passport and student residency papers immediately at the reception triage desk.

## 6. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### Can I cancel my private insurance once I enroll in GeSY?
**No.** Immigration authorities (CRMD) strictly require all non-EU students to maintain an active private hospital insurance policy for annual Pink Slip renewal, regardless of whether you contribute to GeSY through employment.

### Are dental check-ups covered under GeSY?
GeSY covers emergency dental treatments for pain relief and basic preventative care for minors, but routine adult dental services (fillings, root canals, whitening, crowns) must be paid privately.

### How do I locate on-duty pharmacies open overnight or on weekends?
Pharmacies in Cyprus rotate nighttime and weekend shifts. Check the official daily on-duty pharmacy schedule using local apps or the Ministry of Health portal to find open locations in your municipality.
